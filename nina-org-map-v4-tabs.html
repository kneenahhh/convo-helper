Talking to Jamie

You're doing your due diligence. It's one manager's view, not an announcement, so stop waiting for a clear answer and plan around what you control.

Before you go in

Walk out knowing

  1. What Jamie has heard, and her own read
  2. Whether Jamie understands what you've heard and why you're planning
  3. A better read on the probability
  4. Whether she'll open doors, or whether you should start those conversations yourself
Structural risk: real enough to plan for, and unknown in timing or scale.

It's one view, not an announcement. You're doing your due diligence, and your plan continues whatever she says.

The start

Open, ask, and frame

Open

I want to raise something directly, and I'm not asking you to share anything you can't. A manager said, about designers, that it's when, not if, so the role may be phased out. I'm treating that as one view, not a decision. I've also heard senior leaders say they're under NDA on fall plans. So I'm doing my due diligence.

Three questions

  1. Have you heard this too, and what's your own read?
  2. If this were out of your hands, what would you do first?
  3. If the risk is real, would you be willing to help me talk with the right people?

Your frame

I don't see myself as tied to a design title. Most of what I bring is business acumen, systems thinking, enablement strategy, and operations, and I'm open to building more technical depth. The recognition in June meant a lot, and it's why I want to plan early.

Technical depth

What you've been building

Say it simply

I've been building technical depth on real work: watsonx Orchestrate agents in the recent Level 4 labs, Bob to build a React enablement app website, Maximo MAS discovery and readiness for a workshop, and watsonx.data intelligence for a demo bootcamp.

My best technical work has been alongside FDEs and engineers. What I add is the thinking around the build: what the IBM champion at the client needs to evangelize it, and what change management the client needs, so the CIO-level decision maker can sign.

I'd like to aim my next technical growth at [your route]. What would matter most?

Skills you bring to technical rooms

  • Facilitating technical rooms
  • Framing and shaping use cases
  • Mapping workflows and journeys
  • Designing the engagement around the IBM champion, the change management, and the CIO-level decision maker
Your value

You're more than a design role

Short proof she could repeat to others

What you'd want her to say about you. She can't remember everything, so plant this idea in your own words.

She connects business strategy, technical teams, and adoption, keeps building technical depth on real work, and creates ways of working others can use. That value doesn't depend on a design title.

The criteria

Learn what would be measured, if anything

Ask

If leaders were comparing people, what do you think they'd look at?

If there are criteria, I'd like to understand them so I can plan around them.

For your planning

  • You've heard the test may be technical credibility. One manager said this, and Southeast may differ.
  • You're still in high demand. Your work isn't measured as technical, but what you bring takes method, technique, and skills no one else on the team has.
  • You're here to learn the criteria, not to argue that design should stay. If she names one, ask how to meet it.
Routes

Where your value could go next

Ask about routes and support

Name one or two routes at most.

I've been thinking about FDE and Pinnacle Enablement. Which feels more realistic to you? If the risk is real, would you be willing to help me talk with the right people?

Questions for Jamie, on any route

  • If the design role changed, where do you see me adding the most value?
  • Who decides, and who should know my work before that decision?
  • Are internal moves open right now, or is hiring limited?
  • What would you want to see from me in the next 60 days to support a move?
  • Are there other sales or enablement teams you think I should know about?

Background routes

Lines and tone

How to say it

Role, not person

  • A decision like this would be about a role, not anyone's work.
  • I want to avoid something structural deciding my path.
  • I'd rather plan early than be surprised.

If she stays general

  • Is this new to you, or have you heard it too?
  • Would you call this more likely, less likely, or truly unclear?
  • Would it help more for you to make an intro, or for me to reach out?

Staying at IBM

  • I want to keep building here, as a cross-team collaborator and a voice in internal enablement.
  • Who should know my work before a decision is made?

Tone

  • Calm, cool, collected, and confident
  • A little slower than usual
  • Two-beat pause after each question
  • Curious, not concerned
